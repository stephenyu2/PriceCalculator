Child Safety and Boundaries

Why this exists: you work alone with minors. These rules protect students, and they protect you. Most of them are not about preventing bad people from doing bad things. They are about making sure a good tutor is never the only person who can say what happened in a room.

Where sessions happen. In person: anywhere inside the Northridge office building. Suite 105, the upstairs conference room, the cafeteria, the shared areas. Nowhere else. Not a coffee shop, not a park, not a library, not your apartment, not a student's home. If a family asks to meet somewhere else, that request goes through LVT first.

The busier the space, the better. The cafeteria is a better room than a private office, not a worse one. People walking past is the feature, not the problem.

If there's a door, it stays open. The whole session, every session. Not cracked, open. If someone closes it on their way past, open it again. This is not about trust. It is about never being in a position where the only two accounts of what happened are yours and a child's.

Online sessions. We run some sessions on Zoom. Same principle as the open door, achieved a different way.

  • The link goes to the parent or guardian. Never to the student directly. Sending a student a meeting link is private communication with a minor, which is the thing this whole module is about.
  • The parent can join any session, at any time, without telling you first. That is the open door. Do not treat it as an interruption or ask them to schedule it. If a parent asks permission to sit in, the answer is that they never need to ask.
  • Your camera stays on for the entire session. Both of you. A tutor with the camera off is a session with no visibility at all.
  • Take it from somewhere that looks like a workspace. Sitting up, at a desk or table, dressed the way you'd dress for the office. No bed in frame. Not from a car. Not from a coffee shop, where a stranger can hear a student's name and what they're struggling with.
  • Nobody records. Not you, not the student. If a student starts recording, end the session and tell LVT.
  • No private messaging on the platform. Chat during the session is fine. A DM before or after it is not.
  • Zoom only. Not FaceTime, not Discord, not Snapchat, not Google Meet from your personal account, not a personal Zoom room you use for other things.

Never transport a student. Not five minutes down the road. Not in an emergency. Not if the parent asks you to. If a student needs to get somewhere, that is the parent's job or 911's job, not yours.

No private communication. Everything goes through LVT channels or includes a parent. That means: no personal cell texting with a student, no personal email, no Instagram or Snapchat or Discord or DMs of any kind, no gaming platforms, no "just this one time so I can send them the worksheet." If a student contacts you privately, do not respond on that channel. Tell LVT the same day.

No physical contact beyond a handshake or something equally brief and public. If a student initiates contact, redirect gently and report it the same day so it is on the record.

Nothing of value moves between you and a family. No gifts, no money, no rides, no loans, no favors, no letting them buy you lunch. Both directions.

No photos, no video, no recording, no posting. Not of a student, not of a student's work, not of a whiteboard with their name on it, not anonymized, not in a story that disappears. None.

Never impaired. Not alcohol, not cannabis, not anything that affects you, before or during a session or on LVT premises. If you are not sure whether you are okay to teach, you are not okay to teach. Cancel it. A cancelled session is a small problem.

No substitutes without approval. If you cannot make a session, post in the group chat to look for coverage, but the swap is not real until LVT confirms it and the family is told. A different adult showing up unannounced to teach someone's kid is not acceptable no matter how good that adult is.

Report anything that could be misconstrued, including your own conduct. A student hugs you. A parent asks you to stay late alone. A student tells you something heavy. You realize you were alone in a closed room for ten minutes because someone shut the door. Report it the same day. Nobody is in trouble for reporting. The only thing that creates trouble is a gap between when something happened and when anyone heard about it.

If a student gets attached. It happens, and it is not a crisis. Kids get attached to adults who take them seriously. Keep the boundaries exactly where they are, be warm inside them, and tell LVT so we know. Do not manage it privately.
