Session Conduct and Accuracy

Why this exists: a tutor who confidently teaches something wrong does more damage than a tutor who says "I don't know." We have had a student catch a content error mid-session. That is not a small thing.

If you do not know, say you do not know. Out loud. To the student. "I'm not certain, let's work it out" or "let me confirm that and get back to you before next session." This is not weakness. A student watching you look something up carefully is learning something more valuable than the answer.

Never guess with confidence in front of a student. The student writes it down. The student uses it on a test. The parent finds out. Now LVT is the company that taught their kid the wrong thing, and the parent has no way to know which of the other 40 hours were also wrong. One confident wrong answer can cost the entire relationship.

Flag content gaps to Derek. If a topic keeps coming up that you are shaky on, say so. That is a scheduling and materials problem, not a character flaw. What is a problem is quietly winging it.

Do not teach outside what you can actually teach. If a session drifts into a subject you are not qualified for, say so and tell LVT. We would rather move the student than have a bad hour.

Never do the student's work. Not their homework, not their essay, not their college application, not their take-home test. Not "just this part." Not if the parent asks. Not if the student is crying. If a parent or student asks you to, decline in the moment and tell LVT the same day.

Nothing that violates a school's academic integrity rules. No help during a live exam of any kind. If you are not sure whether something crosses the line, it crosses the line until LVT says otherwise.

Show up on time and ready. If you have to cancel, give as much notice as you can, post in the group chat for coverage, and remember the swap is not real until LVT confirms it and the family knows. Unexcused no-shows are the one thing the rate schedule treats as disqualifying at any tier.

Keep your own views out of it. Politics, religion, opinions about a student's school or teachers or family. You are there for the subject.

If you realize you taught something wrong, fix it with the student at the next opportunity and tell Derek the same day. That is a good day, not a bad one. The bad version is the one where you hope nobody noticed.
