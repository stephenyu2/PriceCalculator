Emergencies and Incidents

Why this exists: the difference between a manageable incident and a lawsuit is usually a written record made the same day.

Medical emergency: 911 first. Then Stephen, Mark, or Derek. Then the parent. In that order. Do not call the parent first to ask what to do.

Never administer medication. Not prescription, not over the counter, not the student's own inhaler, not ibuprofen out of your bag, not even if they ask, not even if the parent has said it is fine. Call the parent. If it is an emergency, call 911.

Do not give a student food or drink. You do not know their allergies. Water from a sealed bottle is fine.

Any injury gets an incident report the same day. Any. A scraped knee. A paper cut that bled. A kid who said his stomach hurt. It does not matter that it seemed like nothing. The report costs you four minutes. Not having the report, eighteen months later, when a family's lawyer asks why there is no record, costs a great deal more.

Student not picked up: stay with them. Call the parent. Call LVT. Never leave a minor alone, and never drive them anywhere. If you cannot stay, LVT sends someone. You do not leave until an adult who is supposed to have them arrives.

Student leaves without permission: do not chase them off the property. Call the parent, call LVT, call 911 if they are in danger.

Student is a danger to themselves or someone else: 911. Then LVT. Then the parent. Do not try to manage it alone and do not talk them down instead of calling.

Fire alarm or evacuation at the office: take the student with you, go to the designated meeting point, do not go back in, call the parent from outside.

The incident report is facts, not conclusions. What you saw, what you heard, what you did, in order, with times. Not "the parent was being unreasonable." Not "it wasn't my fault." Write what a camera would have seen. If a student said something, write the words.

Same day. Not "when I get a chance." Your memory at 9pm is evidence. Your memory in three weeks is a liability.
