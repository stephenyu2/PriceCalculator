What AP Chemistry Tutoring Looks Like

AP Chemistry covers a lot of ground — and each unit builds on the last. The tricky part is that the AP exam doesn't just ask you to recall facts. It asks you to explain why something happens, work through multi-step calculations, and write clear free-response answers. Memorizing formulas won't cut it.

At Launch Valley Tutoring in Northridge, tutors work through concepts step by step. They connect ideas across units so students see how everything fits together. They also practice free-response questions the way the College Board actually scores them — because writing a correct answer and writing a scoreable answer aren't always the same thing. Sessions are one-on-one, so the tutor can slow down on whatever is giving the student trouble, without rushing to keep up with a class.

Topics covered include:

  • Atomic structure, electron configuration, and periodicity
  • Chemical bonding and molecular geometry (VSEPR, IMFs)
  • Gases, liquids, solids, and solutions
  • Kinetics and rate laws
  • Equilibrium, Le Chatelier's principle, and ICE tables
  • Acids, bases, and buffers
  • Thermodynamics: enthalpy, entropy, and Gibbs free energy
  • Electrochemistry and redox reactions
  • Free-response writing and multi-step calculation strategy

AP Chemistry Exam Unit Breakdown

The AP Chemistry exam tests nine units with different weightings. Knowing which units carry the most points helps focus study time where it matters most.

Unit Key Topics % of AP Exam
Unit 1 — Atomic Structure & Properties Electron configuration, photoelectron spectroscopy, periodicity 7–9%
Unit 2 — Molecular & Ionic Compound Structure Ionic and covalent bonding, Lewis structures, VSEPR 7–9%
Unit 3 — Intermolecular Forces & Properties IMFs, solids, liquids, solutions, spectroscopy 18–22%
Unit 4 — Chemical Reactions Net ionic equations, oxidation states, reaction types 7–9%
Unit 5 — Kinetics Rate laws, reaction mechanisms, Arrhenius equation 7–9%
Unit 6 — Thermodynamics Enthalpy, Hess's law, bond enthalpies, calorimetry 7–9%
Unit 7 — Equilibrium K expressions, ICE tables, Le Chatelier's principle 7–9%
Unit 8 — Acids & Bases pH, Ka/Kb, buffers, titrations, Henderson-Hasselbalch 11–15%
Unit 9 — Applications of Thermodynamics Entropy, Gibbs free energy, electrochemistry, Nernst equation 7–9%
